Learning to act, together and safely.

I'm an electrical engineering undergraduate working on reinforcement learning for control. The direction is multi-agent: systems of robots that learn to coordinate under limited communication, with guarantees a control engineer would accept.

IIT (BHU) Varanasi · B.Tech EE Robotics Club, junior core seeking research internships

§1 Direction

Most of what I've built so far is single-agent: a policy, an environment and a reward.The multi-agent part is where I'm heading, not where I've published results. Nothing on this site claims otherwise. The questions I keep returning to are the ones that break when you add a second agent, or a hard constraint, or a real actuator: who owns the credit, who owns the safety, and what is the controller still guaranteeing once a learned policy is in the loop.

So the work here is a ladder. Learn the single-agent tools properly, then put them under the conditions that matter: constraints, failures, noisy sensing.Control theory is the reference point throughout. A reward curve is a trend; a control engineer asks for a bound. Coordination comes after.

§2 Questions I'm working on

  1. How do agents reach coordinated behaviour from local information only, and what does a bounded communication graph cost them?
  2. Can a safety constraint be held during learning rather than checked afterward?
  3. Where does the guarantee of a classical controller end and the learned policy begin, and how does the system behave when a component fails?
