Manipulation RL with a 7-DOF arm: where the curriculum stops working

A six-phase curriculum on a simulated KUKA iiwa, from reaching to peg-in-hole, trained with PPO and TQC and evaluated on unseen layouts. The first three phases are solved. Insertion is not, and most of this page is about why.

1Setup

The environment is a 7-DOF KUKA iiwa arm in PyBullet, wrapped in Gymnasium and trained with Stable-Baselines3. The supplied environment was rebuilt around delta joint-position control, a 123-dimensional observation that stacks three frames, and potential-based reward shapingNg, Harada & Russell (1999). Shaping of the form F = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s) leaves the optimal policy unchanged, which is why it is safe to add.. Resets were made 25–30× faster, and the environment is covered by 18 tests.

Every phase adds one difficulty. Each number below is the success rate over 200 episodes on layouts the policy never saw during training, with a deterministic policy.

2The failure

Peg-in-hole sits at 1.5–2% success, with a collision rate above 0.5 (Fig. 1). Training only on the insertion sub-task moved success from 0.040 to 0.073: better, still far from usable. This is a reported negative result, kept in the write-up because it narrows the problem down.

Working hypothesis: shaped distance rewards were enough for the earlier phases, but insertion needs behaviour under contact that the reward never asked for.

This is a hypothesis, not something I've isolated with an ablation. The next step is to test whether the same setup succeeds once contact forces are in the observation.

3Generalisation and noise

The later phases test two things the first four don't: unseen dynamics, and noise injected into observations and actions at evaluation time.Because phase 4 and beyond never reached good in-distribution performance, there is little for these tests to degrade. The curves are mainly useful as a sanity check, and the cleaner experiment needs a solved task to start from.

4PPO against TQC

TQCTruncated Quantile Critics (Kuznetsov et al., ICML 2020), an off-policy method that truncates the upper quantiles of the critic distribution to curb overestimation. is the more sample-efficient algorithm in principle, but here it cost far more wall-clock time and did not win on the task I could compare.

5What I would change

  • Add contact-force or tactile channels to the observation and test insertion again.
  • Run the dynamics-generalisation test on a task that is already solved, so a drop means something.
  • Repeat the PPO against TQC comparison on phases 2 and 3, not only reaching.
