Safe RL navigation: three ways to hold a constraint

A robot has to reach a goal without touching obstacles. The experiment is about how the safety requirement enters the learning: as a penalty, as a constrained optimisation, or as a barrier condition. This page is a design and a status report. There are no training results yet.

CodeGitHub ↗

1Environment

A disc robot of radius 0.15 m in an 8 × 8 m arena with up to eight obstacles (radius 0.2–0.5 m). The observation is 21-dimensional and includes 16 rangefinder rays; the action is a 2-D velocity command. Physics steps at 2 ms and control at 50 ms, with 400-step episodes.

2Three formulations

Every phase uses the same learner, so differences come from how safety is encoded.

  1. Penalty. Shape the reward directly:
    r′ = r − k · 1[collision](1)
    Simple, but the constraint is only as strong as the weight k.
  2. PPO-Lagrangian. Treat collisions as a cost with a budget d and learn a multiplier by dual ascent:
    maxπ Jr(π)   s.t.   Jc(π) ≤ d(2)
    The multiplier λ rises while the budget is exceeded, so the weight is learned and not hand-tuned.
  3. Barrier cost. Penalise violation of a discrete-time barrier condition on a clearance function h:
    h(xt+1) − h(xt) ≥ −α h(xt)(3)
    Unlike (1) and (2), this penalises approaching an obstacle too fast before any collision has happened.This is the discrete-time control barrier function condition from the safe-control literature. Here it is used as a training signal, not as a hard filter on actions, so it does not give a guarantee by itself.

4Status

The environment exists and the actuator model checks out. step(), the reward and all training are not written yet. When results exist they will replace this section, with the failures included.

Why this matters for what comes next

Holding a constraint during learning is also the single-agent version of a multi-agent problem: agents that must not collide with each other while they learn to coordinate. This is the stepping stone.
