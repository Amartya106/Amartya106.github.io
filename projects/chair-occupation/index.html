Chair occupancy detection on a deliberately small dataset

Detecting whether a specific chair is free, in real time, from a handful of training images, and choosing the architecture on evidence rather than on version number.

1The goal

A room-level occupancy sensor that needs no sensor in the furniture: point a camera at a space and have it report which chairs are free, eventually running on an Arduino UNO Q rather than a workstation.

The current model detects the unoccupied chair reliably. The harder class, a chair with someone in it, is still in progress, and I'd rather say so than round it up.

2Choosing the architecture

The obvious move was the newest YOLO release. I trained both and compared them instead, and the newer version lost.

ModelF1 peak confidenceBounding boxesChosen
YOLOv80.644Tighter, more consistentYes
YOLOv110.357Looser on the target objectNo

YOLOv8 peaks at a high confidence threshold, so the model is genuinely certain about its correct detections. YOLOv11 peaking at 0.357 means it reaches its best precision–recall balance only by accepting predictions it isn't confident in, a more fragile operating point when the lighting changes.

The threshold is a design decision, not a default. Reading it off the F1 curve rather than leaving it at 0.25 is the difference between a demo and something you'd deploy.

3On that 99.5%

It's a real number, and it's a small dataset of one specific chair. It says the training pipeline and labels are sound; it does not say the model would survive a different room. This is a well-fit detector on a narrow problem, and the next step is widening the problem.
