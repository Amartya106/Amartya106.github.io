About

Electrical Engineering at IIT (BHU) Varanasi.

I'm building toward robot learning — systems that perceive their surroundings, decide what to do, and move safely while doing it.

Approach

I came to robotics through electrical engineering, which shapes how I approach it: I want to know why something works before I trust it. That's why the neural network came before PyTorch, and why I'm working through Ogata rather than treating control as a solved black box behind a library call.

The direction I'm heading is the intersection of perception, control and learning. Reinforcement learning can find behaviours nobody hand-designed; control theory tells you when a system will stay stable. Real robots need both, and the interesting problems live in the tension between them.

I learn by building something physical and letting it fail. A servo that freezes mid-gesture teaches you about blocking loops faster than any chapter on real-time systems, and a detector at 99.5% on one chair teaches you what a benchmark number is and isn't worth.
